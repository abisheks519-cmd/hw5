Ticket 101 · Customer order · open

Bulldog tee

Requester

Linked record

Received
2026-08-31, 9:15 AM ET

“Needs a tee in size S.”

Expected

Plan

A customer wants one white tee in size S, and size S is out of stock. So the job is to learn what the shop can offer and tell the customer honestly. Only two specialists are needed: Inventory for the facts, then Customer Service for the reply.

Boss→Inventory→ back to Boss →Customer Service→ back to Boss

Who the Boss calls first, and why

Inventory. Everything on this ticket depends on one fact: is a size S tee on the shelf? Until Inventory checks, there is nothing true to tell the customer, so Customer Service can't write a useful reply yet.

Inventory is also the right agent for the linked invoice 501. That invoice is a rush reprint of this exact shirt, and Inventory can read it with get_invoice to explain what it means for restocking.

Every delegation, in order

  1. Boss reads the ticket not a hand-off
    What it does
    Calls get_ticket(101) and sees a customer order for one CC-TEE-WHITE in size S, linked to invoice 501.
  2. Boss → Inventory
    Ask
    “Ticket 101: is CC-TEE-WHITE size S on hand? If not, which sizes are, and what does linked invoice 501 say about restocking?”
    Responsibility
    Get the stock facts and the restock picture. Size S at 0 counts as a reorder under the shop rules, but Inventory only reports it and doesn't decide anything about paying the invoice.
    Comes back with
    Size S: 0 on hand. Sizes M (5), L (3), and XL (2) are in stock. Invoice 501 is an $840 rush reprint from Bulldog Print Co. It is still open, was due 2026-08-28 (3 days overdue), and has a 5-day lead time, but there is no delivery date.
  3. Boss → Customer Service
    Ask
    “Draft a reply to Tauhid for ticket 101. Facts from Inventory: size S is out of stock; M, L, and XL are available; no restock date.”
    Responsibility
    Write a short, friendly draft that follows the shop rules. It says size S is out of stock, offers M, L, or XL if one works, and promises a follow-up within 1 business day. It gives no restock date, and no mention of the vendor, the invoice, or the aisle.
    Comes back with
    A reply draft for a person to review and send. Nothing is sent automatically.
  4. Boss records the result not a hand-off
    What it does
    Calls update_ticket(101) with status waiting_on_approval and a short note.
    Why that status
    The customer is waiting on stock, and restocking depends on the overdue $840 reprint invoice, which only the owner can decide to pay. When both "waiting" statuses apply, the owner's decision comes first.

Ticket 102 · Rent notice · open

Rent due

Requester
Elm City Properties (the landlord)
Linked record
Lease 1
Received
2026-08-31, 11:05 AM ET

“Email: shop rent due in 2 days.”

Expected

Plan

The landlord says rent is due in two days, so this is a building-and-money ticket. Facilities checks the notice against the lease. Accounting checks the cash and pays only if a person approved payments for the run. No customer is involved.

Boss→Facilities→Accounting→ back to Facilities → back to Boss

Who the Boss calls first, and why

Facilities. Facilities owns the lease and the landlord. The first job is to check the email against the lease record (the amount, the due date, and who gets paid) instead of trusting the email. No money should move until the bill is confirmed.

Facilities can't pay bills, so it brings in Accounting itself. That keeps the order right: confirm the bill first, then deal with the money.

Every delegation, in order

  1. Boss reads the ticket not a hand-off
    What it does
    Calls get_ticket(102) and sees a rent notice from Elm City Properties, linked to lease 1.
  2. Boss → Facilities
    Ask
    “Ticket 102: check lease 1 against this rent notice and get the rent handled.”
    Responsibility
    Confirm the bill is real and matches the lease, get it paid through Accounting, and draft a short note to the landlord if one is needed.
    Finds
    With get_rent_due(1): Chapel Street shop, Elm City Properties, $2,400, due 2026-09-02. That is 2 days after the shop date (2026-08-31), so it matches the email.
  3. Facilities → Accounting
    Ask
    “Please pay the $2,400 rent for lease 1, due 2026-09-02.”
    Responsibility
    Check the cash and the order bills should be paid in, then pay (with approval) or list the payment for the owner (without).
    Finds
    With get_cash_position: $3,400 in checking, the overdue $840 invoice 501, and $2,400 rent, which would leave $160 if both were paid. The shop rules put rent first. Paying rent alone leaves $1,000, above the $100 floor and the $500 warning line.
    Without approval
    No payment. Adds “Pay $2,400 rent for lease 1” to approvals needed, and mentions invoice 501 is also overdue.
    With approval
    When the run uses --approve-payments NAME, it calls record_payment(rent, lease 1, $2,400). Cash goes from $3,400 to $1,000, and the next rent due date moves to 2026-10-02. It pays only the rent, because that's what the ticket is about.
  4. Accounting → Facilities → Boss reports come back
    What happens
    Facilities adds Accounting's answer to its report, plus a short landlord draft. The draft confirms the amount and due date, and says “paid” only if Accounting shows a receipt.
  5. Boss records the result not a hand-off
    What it does
    Calls update_ticket(102).
    Status
    waiting_on_approval if no one approved payments. resolved if Accounting returns a payment receipt.

3

One agent vs. five

1

Where one agent would have been simpler

  • Ticket 102 is basically a straight line: read the ticket, check the lease, check cash, prepare rent. That's 5 tool calls, but the team used 8 tool calls and 11 model requests because every hand-off starts a new agent that has to read its own long instructions.
  • The invoice 501 miss came from a hand-off. The agent that saw the bill couldn't act on money, and the one that could was never asked. One agent with get_invoice and prepare_payment would have seen both.
  • More agents meant more chances for the gateway to hang, which is why 103 took 20 minutes.
5

Where having five agents helped

  • Customer Service doesn't have cash or invoice tools, and none of the replies mentioned the $22 cost, the $840 invoice, or shelf locations.
  • Only Accounting and Inventory can prepare money moves, and neither can approve them, so cash only moved twice, both times when I clicked.
  • On 102, Facilities checked the notice before Accounting touched any money, which is a good control.
  • The audit trail and the contribution cards make it easy to see who did what.

Overall, I think the multi-agent setup is worth it for the controls (privacy, who can touch money, checking before paying), not because it's smarter. For these three tickets, one agent probably would have been cheaper and faster. The bigger fix is better hand-offs when an agent notices a money problem.

Three problems the team couldn't solve

ideas, not run

Booking the club's order if they say yes.

Nothing can record a sale, take payment, or reduce stock, which is why the Cash tab shows $0 in inflows.

It would need an orders table, a record_sale tool, a payment processor connection, and a Sales/Fulfillment agent.

“When will my size S tee arrive?”

The team only knows a 5-day lead time. There's no way to track purchase orders or mark stock as received.

It would need a purchase_orders table, a vendor ETA tool, a receive_shipment tool, and a Receiving agent (or that job added to Inventory).

Actually emailing customers and the landlord.

Right now everything is a draft I have to send myself, and we don't store any contact info.

It would need a contacts table, an outbox tool, a human approve-send step like the one for payments, an inbox tool, and a Communications agent.
